Research Interests

My research area is 3P Vision: Pixels, Perception and Physics of Computer Vision. Primarily, I explore the science aspect of modern computer vision. I aim to bridge classical perception theories with modern generative models, advancing computer vision, computer graphics and computational photography. A central focus of my research is driven by scientific curiosity to understand how generative models compare to biological perceptual systems and to investigate whether these models have learned novel perceptual laws distinct from those known in traditional vision science. I explore where and how perceptual and physical world knowledge is encoded in generative models and how it can be extracted. I believe this will help us interpret and build better generative models that are rooted in real-world physics.

My long-term goal is to develop methods that rely on the physical world to inform their understanding, without depending on labeled data. For generative models to truly become world models, they need to understand more than just how to generate pixels -- they need to grasp the principles of perception and physics. For a considerable long period, I'll be working towards pushing these models beyond simple image generation, helping them internalize the rules that govern the world around us.

My notable recent contributions include the recovery of intrinsic images[1, 2] and finding lighting controls[3] encoded within generative models, revealing their limitations in adhering to projective geometry[4]. Further, my research demonstrates the emergence of latent[5] from training to relight, challenging traditional intrinsic image decompositions.

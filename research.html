Research Interests

My research focuses on the intersection of pixels, perception, and physics, which I colloquially call 3P Vision. My group at Johns Hopkins is the 3P Vision Group.

I want to build AI models that perceive, recreate, and eventually physically interact with the visual world, learning as much as possible from raw images and video. One question sits at the center of this inquiry: what do models trained on pixels already know about the world? My group probes that knowledge, finds where it breaks, and works to improve it. Just as important is knowing when pixels are not enough, and when a problem needs touch, physical simulation, or other measurements, from robot manipulation to computational mechanics.

Pixels (I like pretty pixels!)

  • Controllable generative models that are easy to use: Generative Cinematographer, Thinking in Boxes, Generative Blocks World
  • Rendering and relighting: SyncLight, LumiNet, StyLitGAN, ZeroComp, SpotLight

Perception

  • Knowledge in generative models: StyleGAN Knows, Intrinsic LoRA, Shadows Don't Lie
  • 3D and lighting perception: Latent Intrinsics, OmniRay, GenWildSplat, SyncFix, DIVeR, Improving Equivariance
  • Vision architectures: ViT-5, RATS!

Physics

  • Physically grounded models and computational mechanics: Principia, Objects Are Slower Than They Appear, FracGen, PowerSim
  • Multisensory perception for robot learning: OpenTac, Do You Know Where Your Camera Is?
Why should we care about 3P Vision?

For decades, computer vision has been guided by what Malik et al., called the Three R’s: Recognition, Reconstruction and Reorganization. We have made remarkable progress on all three and some would even argue they are nearly solved. Many things we struggled to do a few years ago can now be done with an off-the-shelf model. So, what’s next for computer vision? Do we all become roboticists, as many in our community are doing or is there a broader direction to explore?

My group works on robots as well, though I think there's a more fundamental question beneath it all: do our models actually understand the world that they're trained on? That's the question that 3P Vision is trying to answer. The state of the art in today's models is that they can produce images and videos that look correct at the pixel level. Looking correct at the pixel level is not the same as being correct in any perceptual or physical sense, though. This is crucial since the model might err easily if it fails to understand the concepts of gravity, geometry, or light in the ever-changing environment or through unintended assumptions.

Thus, 3P Vision views pixels, perception, and physics as three layers of reasoning. The model must go from the pixels to perception, and further to the physics of the world. The testing of each layer separately will reveal its weaknesses and thus the way forward.

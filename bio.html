About Me

I am an incoming Assistant Professor in the Department of Computer Science at Johns Hopkins University. Currently, I am a Research Assistant Professor (RAP) at the Toyota Technological Institute at Chicago (TTIC), a nonprofit computer science institute. The RAP role, akin to a postdoctoral position, allows for independent, faculty-like responsibilities. You can read about my experience as an RAP.

I spent the summer of 2024 as a visiting scholar at UC Berkeley, hosted by Alexei (Alyosha) Efros. Earlier, I completed my PhD from Computer Vision Group in the Department of Computer Science at the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign (UIUC), where my advisor was David Forsyth.

Before my PhD, I completed dual master’s degrees in Computer Science and Civil and Environmental Engineering at UIUC and a bachelor’s degree in Civil Engineering from NITK Surathkal, India.


My research contributions primarily include revealing how generative models can learn complex physical knowledge. I discovered classical computer vision concepts like intrinsic images—decomposing scenes into color, shape, and lighting—emerge within these models without explicit training[1, 2, 3]. I also uncovered fundamental limitations in their understanding of projective geometry, providing reliable detection signatures for generated images[4]. These findings led to state-of-the-art indoor scene relighting methods[5, 6] and demonstrated 3D scene synthesis from single images using the largest open-sourced 360° video dataset[7].

Beyond research, I designed and taught Past Meets Present: A Tale of Two Visions at TTIC, exploring the intersections between classical computer vision and modern deep learning. I also lead community-building initiatives and organize workshops at conferences like CVPR and ECCV.

About Me

I am an Assistant Professor in the Department of Computer Science at Johns Hopkins University.

Earlier, I was a Research Assistant Professor (RAP) at the Toyota Technological Institute at Chicago (TTIC). In Summer 2024, I was a visiting scholar at UC Berkeley, hosted by Alexei (Alyosha) Efros. I earned my PhD from the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign (UIUC), where I was advised by David Forsyth. I have also been fortunate to be mentored by Derek Hoiem, Svetlana Lazebnik, Greg Shakhnarovich, and Shenlong Wang.

Before my PhD, I completed dual master’s degrees in Computer Science and Civil and Environmental Engineering at UIUC and a bachelor’s degree in Civil Engineering from NITK Surathkal, India.


My research spans computer vision, generative modeling, and physical reasoning. My research contributions primarily include revealing how generative models can learn complex physical knowledge. I discovered classical computer vision concepts like intrinsic images—decomposing scenes into color, shape, and lighting—emerge within these models without explicit training[1, 2, 3]. I also identified fundamental limitations in their understanding of projective geometry, providing reliable detection signatures for generated images[4]. These findings led to state-of-the-art indoor scene relighting methods[5, 6] and enabled 3D scene synthesis from a single image using the largest open-source 360° video dataset[7]. A talk on my research is available here.

Beyond research, I designed and taught Past Meets Present: A Tale of Two Visions at TTIC, exploring the intersections between classical computer vision and modern deep learning. I also lead community-building initiatives and organize workshops at conferences like CVPR and ECCV.
